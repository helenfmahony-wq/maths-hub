How it was worked out

Teacher notes

Quartile method. This tool uses the "median of each half" method. When n is odd, the median is left out of both halves. Spreadsheet functions such as Excel's QUARTILE.INC use interpolation, so they can give slightly different answers. That difference can make a good discussion point.

Suggested uses

  • Two views: The Dot plot tab uses whole numbers from 0 to 50 and lets students drag values. The List of numbers tab shows the data unsorted, so students have to put it in order themselves. Add a random number adds a whole number near the existing data. Outside quiz mode, Put in order sorts the list. In quiz mode it becomes Check order: students type their ordered list and get feedback on which positions are wrong. Each tab keeps its own data.
  • Predict, then reveal: Switch to Student quiz mode and have students calculate the summary by hand before checking it. Once their five-number summary is correct, students draw the box plot by dragging handles (Step 2) and check it. Next question gives a fresh random dataset. Switch back with Teaching mode at the top.
  • Odd vs even n: Add or remove one value and discuss why the median and quartiles sometimes land on a value and sometimes between two values.
  • Range vs IQR: Press "Add an outlier" and compare how much each measure changes. Why is the IQR described as more resistant to extreme values?
  • Shape: Load the skewed datasets and link the tail of the dot plot to the longer whisker and the uneven sides of the box.
  • Challenge: "Build a dataset of 10 values with a median of 8 and an IQR of 4." Or: "Can you change the range without changing the IQR?"
